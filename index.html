Health Risks and Pathogen Exposure

Blood and bodily fluids contain various pathogens that can transmit serious diseases to anyone exposed without proper protection. Hepatitis B and hepatitis C are particularly common in blood-borne pathogens and can survive on surfaces for extended periods. Human immunodeficiency virus can be transmitted through contact with blood or certain bodily fluids. Methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus, a antibiotic-resistant bacteria, is frequently found in blood contamination. The risk of infection increases if a person has cuts, sores, or breaks in their skin that allow pathogens to enter the body. Even if no visible injury exists, mucous membranes in the nose, mouth, and eyes can provide entry points for harmful microorganisms. Our Longmont team understands these risks completely and uses protective equipment rated for bloodborne pathogen exposure. We never take shortcuts when handling contaminated materials, as inadequate protection can have serious health consequences. Families should absolutely not attempt to clean blood themselves due to these significant infection risks.
